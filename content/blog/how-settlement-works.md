## Collection and settlement are two different events

A customer paying for something and a merchant actually receiving that money are two separate events, often separated by hours or days. Collection is when the customer's payment clears. Settlement is when that money is transferred into the merchant's own bank account, ready to be used.

Confusing the two is a common source of cash flow surprises — a merchant seeing a payment marked "successful" isn't the same as that money being available to spend yet.

## Why settlement isn't instant by default

Settlement takes time because of how banking systems clear funds between institutions. A standard settlement window across African markets is T+1 for verified merchant accounts — funds collected today become available the next business day. This isn't arbitrary; it reflects the underlying bank clearing cycles that a payment provider has to work within, market by market.

International payouts add another layer, since they typically route through intermediary banks, which is why cross-border settlement can take two to three business days depending on the destination.

## Settlement schedules matter more than settlement speed

For most merchants, the more useful question isn't "how fast can settlement possibly be" but "how predictable is it." A business with tight margins needs to know exactly when funds will land so it can plan supplier payments, payroll, or inventory purchases around it.

That's why flexible settlement schedules — daily, weekly, or on-demand withdrawal above a set threshold — tend to matter more in practice than shaving a few hours off a single settlement cycle.

## Multi-currency settlement adds a reconciliation layer

Settling in NGN, GHS, KES, ZAR, or any other African currency means every settlement batch potentially needs its own currency conversion, at its own rate, on its own day. Without automated reconciliation, matching settled amounts back to the original transactions — accounting for FX — becomes a manual accounting exercise that gets harder as volume grows.

Automated reconciliation that matches every settlement to its source transactions, and exports the result as CSV or PDF, is what keeps finance teams from having to rebuild that matching logic themselves every month.

## What to check before relying on a settlement schedule

Before committing to a payment provider, it's worth confirming three things directly: the actual settlement window (not just "fast settlement" as marketing language), whether the schedule can be adjusted to daily, weekly, or on-demand, and how reconciliation is delivered. Those three answers tell a merchant more about real-world cash flow than any speed claim on its own.
