You're selling across African markets. Customers are placing orders, payments are coming in, and your sales numbers may look healthy.

But the amount your business ultimately retains can tell a different story.

A payment can fail before an order is completed, reducing the sales value you convert. A successful payment can then lose value through processing costs, FX, payouts, refunds, disputes or settlement-related deductions.

For cross-border e-commerce, payment performance therefore affects both sides of the margin equation: how much order value you successfully convert and how much of each completed sale you ultimately retain.

The first step is to identify where value is being lost. From there, you can determine whether the problem is payment conversion, total payment cost, settlement, market coverage or your current payment setup.

## Key Takeaways

- Payment cost extends beyond the processing fee. FX, payouts, refunds, disputes, settlement and operational work can reduce retained value.
- Failed payments put potential order value at risk before any payment cost is incurred.
- A lower processing fee is not necessarily more profitable if the payment route results in more failed or abandoned transactions.
- Payment coverage should be assessed by market, including relevant payment methods, currencies, merchant eligibility and settlement.
- Successfully collected funds are not necessarily immediately usable cash.
- The strongest payment setup is the one that helps you convert more orders while retaining more value from completed sales.

## Where Is Your E-commerce Payment Setup Losing Value?

Your payment setup can reduce the value of your e-commerce operation either by preventing customers from completing purchases or by reducing what you retain after a successful transaction.

### Before the sale: payment conversion

Customers can abandon a purchase when their preferred payment method is unavailable, a payment route fails repeatedly or a transaction remains pending for too long.

The commercial question is not simply how many payments failed. It is how much potential order value those failures put at risk and how much of that value you were able to recover through retries or alternative payment methods.

### After the sale: retained value

A completed payment does not necessarily equal the amount your business ultimately keeps.

Processing fees, FX costs, payout charges, refunds, disputes and settlement-related deductions can reduce the value of a successful transaction.

Your payment review should therefore answer two questions:

1. How much order value are we successfully converting?
2. How much value do we retain from those completed sales?

## What Is the True Cost of a Cross-Border E-commerce Payment?

The true cost of a cross-border payment is the difference between what the customer pays and what your business ultimately retains and can use after the relevant payment and settlement costs.

The processing fee is only one part of that calculation.

### Processing fees

Providers may charge a percentage, a fixed fee or both. Small differences become significant when applied across thousands of transactions.

### Currency conversion

When the customer's payment currency differs from your settlement currency, the exchange rate, FX spread and conversion charges can affect the final amount received.

### Payout and settlement costs

Moving or settling funds can introduce additional charges depending on the payment and settlement arrangement. Settlement timing also affects when the money becomes available to your business.

### Refunds and disputes

Refunds, chargebacks and reversals can create costs beyond the original transaction. Review how fees, FX costs and other deductions are handled when a transaction is reversed.

### Operational cost

Your finance and operations teams may also spend time matching payments against orders, settlement reports, refunds and accounting records.

A payment route can therefore look inexpensive at checkout while becoming more expensive across the full transaction lifecycle.

## How Much Order Value Are Failed Payments Putting at Risk?

Failed payments represent potential order value that did not become a completed sale, although some of that value may be recovered through retries or alternative payment methods.

For example, suppose your business has:

- 10,000 payment attempts
- 800 failed attempts
- $75 average order value

Those failures represent:

800 × $75 = $60,000 of attempted order value

That does not mean your business definitely lost $60,000. Some customers may retry, use another payment method, return later or complete the purchase through another route.

The more useful question is: How much of the failed order value could realistically have been recovered?

Track:

- failed-payment value
- payment completion rate
- successful retries
- abandoned payment attempts
- failure rate by market and payment method
- customers who complete later
- pending payment volume

This separates temporary payment friction from order value your business is genuinely failing to convert.

## How Should You Evaluate Payment Coverage Across African Markets?

Evaluate payment coverage market by market to determine whether customers can pay using relevant methods and whether your business can receive and settle those payments appropriately.

For each priority market, establish:

What to check Why it matters

| Customer payment methods | Determines whether customers can pay using methods relevant to that market |
|---|---|
| Currency | Establishes what customers pay and what your business ultimately receives |
| Merchant Eligibility | Confirms whether your business can actually onboard and operate through the provider |
| Settlement | Determines where, when and in what currency funds become available |
| Refunds & Disputes | Confirms whether post-payment issues can be managed appropriately |
| Transaction Limit | Establishes whether the route can support your payment volume and transaction sizes |

One distinction is particularly important: **c**ustomer-country support and merchant-country eligibility are not the same thing.

A provider may support customers in a particular country without offering the merchant onboarding, settlement structure or payment configuration your business requires.

## What Should You Check Before Choosing a Cross-Border Payment Provider?

Choose a payment provider based on the result it can deliver for your e-commerce operation, not simply its headline processing fee.

At minimum, compare:

- Payment performance: Can customers complete payments reliably across your priority markets and methods?
- Total cost: What do processing, FX, payouts, refunds, disputes and other deductions leave you with?
- Settlement: How quickly and predictably can you access the funds from completed sales?
- Operational fit: Can your team monitor, reconcile and manage payment activity without unnecessary manual work?
- Market fit: Can the setup support the markets and payment methods your growth strategy requires?

The final comparison should answer one question: Which payment setup gives us the strongest combination of completed sales, retained value, cash availability and manageable operations?

## Is Your Payment Setup Protecting Both Conversion and Margin?

A payment setup is commercially strong when it helps customers complete purchases while allowing your business to retain and access the resulting value efficiently.

Review your current operation for signs such as:

- high payment failure rates in important markets
- significant order value failing at checkout
- weak recovery after payment failures
- payment costs that are higher than expected after FX and other deductions
- long periods between successful payment and usable funds
- excessive reconciliation or payment-support work
- market expansion being limited by payment-method or settlement gaps

If several of these problems appear together, the issue may not be the processing fee alone. Your current payment setup may be limiting both sales conversion and retained value.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the biggest payment cost for cross-border e-commerce merchants in Africa?

The biggest cost depends on the merchant's payment mix and operating model. It can come from processing fees, FX conversion, failed transactions, payouts, disputes, settlement deductions or the operational work required to manage payments. Compare total retained value rather than relying on the advertised processing rate.

### How do payment failures affect e-commerce revenue?

Payment failures can reduce revenue by preventing customers from completing otherwise viable purchases. The commercial impact depends on failure volume, average order value, retry recovery and whether customers abandon the purchase completely.

### How should merchants compare African payment providers?

Compare payment completion, total cost, market and payment-method fit, settlement and operational requirements rather than processing fees alone. The strongest provider is the one that produces the best commercial result for the merchant's actual markets and transaction profile.

### Does supporting a customer country mean a merchant can operate there?

No. Customer-country payment support and merchant eligibility are separate questions. A provider may support customers in a country without providing the onboarding, settlement or payment configuration your business requires.

### How important is settlement when choosing a payment provider?

Settlement is important because successfully collected funds are not necessarily immediately usable funds. Confirm settlement timing, currency, destination and any holds, reserves or deductions that could affect the value and availability of your funds.
