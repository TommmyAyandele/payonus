## A chargeback is the last step, not the first sign

By the time a chargeback lands, a merchant has already lost the sale, the goods or service, and often a processing fee on top. The actual opportunity to prevent fraud is earlier — in the signals a transaction gives off before it's even completed.

Unusual patterns tend to look similar across markets: a sudden spike in transaction volume from a single customer, a cluster of declined attempts from the same card in quick succession, or payments originating from a location that doesn't match a customer's usual behaviour. None of these guarantee fraud on their own, but together they're the raw material that risk scoring is built from.

## Real-time visibility changes what's actually preventable

Fraud caught after settlement is fraud that's already cost money. Fraud caught in the seconds between authorisation and settlement is fraud that can still be stopped. That difference is entirely about how quickly risk signals reach a decision point — which is why real-time dashboards and alerts matter more for fraud prevention than end-of-day reports ever will.

A merchant that only reviews fraud patterns weekly is, by definition, always reacting to fraud that already happened rather than catching it in progress.

## Friction is a trade-off, not a free win

It's tempting to respond to fraud risk by adding more verification steps to every transaction — but every additional step is also a point where a legitimate customer can abandon a purchase. The better approach is layered: apply stronger checks (like 3D Secure authentication) selectively, to transactions that already carry risk signals, rather than uniformly to every customer.

This is why risk scoring exists as a discipline — it's what allows friction to be applied where it's actually needed instead of everywhere.

## Handling a dispute once it happens

Even with strong prevention, disputes happen. What separates a well-handled dispute from a costly one is usually documentation: having a clear transaction record, delivery or service confirmation, and communication history ready to submit within the card network's evidence window. Chargeback rules typically give a merchant a matter of days to respond — missing that window is often what turns a disputable chargeback into an automatic loss.

## What this means operationally

Fraud prevention isn't a single feature — it's the combination of real-time transaction visibility, risk-based authentication, and having dispute evidence ready before it's needed. Merchants evaluating payment infrastructure should ask specifically how risk signals are surfaced, not just whether "fraud protection" is offered as a line item.
