## Drivers choose platforms based on how fast they get paid

In most markets, drivers and riders work across multiple ride-hailing or delivery platforms at once, switching between whichever is busiest or pays out fastest that day. A platform that settles earnings same-day has a direct retention advantage over one that makes drivers wait — payout speed isn't a back-office detail here, it's a competitive factor in driver supply.

That's a different dynamic from most payment use cases, where the person being paid doesn't have the option to simply drive for a competitor instead.

## Every fare is really a multi-party payment

A single ride or delivery fee usually needs to be split at least three ways: the driver's earnings, the platform's commission, and sometimes a third-party partner's share. Doing that split manually, transaction by transaction, doesn't scale past a handful of drivers — it needs to happen automatically, on every transaction, without an operations team reconciling percentages by hand.

Split payment infrastructure that routes each party's share automatically is what turns this from a spreadsheet problem into something that just works in the background.

## Cash still matters, and it complicates reconciliation

Cash-on-delivery and cash fares remain common in many African markets, which means most ride-hailing and logistics platforms are running two parallel payment systems — digital and cash — and need a single reconciliation view across both. Treating cash as a separate, manually-tracked category rather than folding it into the same reconciliation system is where a lot of operational accounting errors originate.

## Bulk payouts are an operational necessity, not a convenience

Paying a few drivers individually is manageable. Paying hundreds or thousands of drivers at the end of a payout cycle, one transaction at a time, is not — it's slow, error-prone, and expensive in processing fees. Bulk payout processing, where an entire batch of drivers gets paid in a single operation, is what makes payout day operationally survivable at any real scale.

## What ride-hailing and logistics platforms should prioritise

The infrastructure priorities here are specific: payout speed (ideally same-day), automatic split payments across driver, platform, and partner shares, multi-channel fare collection that includes the payment methods drivers and riders actually use locally, and reconciliation that accounts for cash alongside digital payments rather than treating it as a separate system.
