## Payments are regulated market by market

Unlike a lot of software, payment processing can't be shipped first and licensed later. Every African market that a payment provider operates in has its own central bank or financial regulator, its own licensing category for who is allowed to move customer funds, and its own rules for how that money has to be safeguarded.

For a merchant, this usually stays invisible — until something goes wrong with an unlicensed or under-regulated provider, at which point it becomes the merchant's problem too, not just the provider's.

## What licensing actually protects

A payment license isn't a formality. It typically requires a provider to demonstrate capital adequacy, submit to regular audits, and ring-fence customer funds separately from its own operating accounts. That separation is what protects a merchant's money if a payment provider ever runs into financial trouble — funds held on behalf of merchants aren't available to creditors the way a provider's own assets would be.

Checking whether a payment provider is actually licensed by the relevant central bank — not just claiming to be "compliant" — is one of the simplest due-diligence steps a merchant can take before integrating.

## Data protection is part of compliance, not separate from it

Processing a payment means handling personal and financial data — card details, bank account numbers, transaction history. Data protection regulation across Africa increasingly treats this data with the same seriousness as the money itself, requiring encryption in transit and at rest, strict retention limits, and clear processes for reporting a breach if one occurs.

A provider that treats security certifications (like ISO 27001 or PCI-DSS) as a checkbox rather than an operating discipline is a provider whose compliance posture is worth questioning further.

## Why this matters for merchant selection

Compliance isn't the most exciting part of choosing a payment provider, but it's the part with the most asymmetric downside if ignored. A cheaper, faster integration with an unlicensed provider can look attractive right up until a regulatory action freezes settlement, or a data breach becomes a merchant's problem to explain to its own customers.

The practical takeaway: ask a payment provider directly which regulator licenses them, what certifications they hold, and how customer funds are safeguarded — before signing anything.
